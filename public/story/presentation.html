2–3 Minute Talk

Creative Process

1

The idea

"ISKRA" is a mystery set at the University of Ruse "Angel Kanchev," on the Danube. A professor vanishes the night her research becomes too dangerous to keep quiet, and her student has to find her — and uncover a cover-up — before the river floods again. I wanted the setting (a real Bulgarian university, the real Danube) to feel grounded, while keeping every character fictional, so I could invent flaws, secrets, and conflict freely without depicting any real person.

2

How I used AI tools

  • Brainstorming at scale: three alternative characters and locations generated before picking one each.
  • Structural planning: three plot structures compared before choosing a classic three-act investigation.
  • Drafting and expansion: a full first draft, then targeted scene expansions rather than generic padding.
  • Image prompt engineering: structured prompts (style, lighting, composition, mood, camera, art style) for every character and location.
  • Web build: page structure, navigation, and a clean editorial visual theme.
3

What I changed manually

I chose which alternative to keep for each character, location, and plot structure. I flagged early that using real named professors with invented personalities was an ethical problem for a fictional mystery, and redirected the entire cast to be original inventions set against the real university backdrop instead. I also caught and fixed a few consistency slips on a full re-read — a prop that didn't physically make sense, an ambiguous reference to two different events sharing a name, and a timeline mismatch in the flood's arrival — before treating the story as final.

4

What worked well

Building alternatives first, then choosing, made the final story feel intentional. Tying recurring visual details — the compass, the cipher notebook, the gear-shaped cufflinks — into both the prose and the image prompts kept the whole project consistent across mediums. The three-act structure mapped cleanly onto three real locations, which made the site's Beginning/Middle/End navigation feel natural.

5

What was difficult

Balancing "real" and "fictional" — using a real university and river as atmosphere while making sure no real living person was recognizable in the cast. Hitting the word count without padding was also a challenge; the fix was expanding character-driven beats rather than adding filler description.

6

What I learned

Alternatives-then-refine is a stronger AI workflow than asking for one "final" version up front. Ethical judgment calls are still mine to make — AI can flag a risk, but the direction has to come from me. And keeping one consistent visual motif across the story, the image prompts, and the site design is what makes a multi-format project feel like a single coherent piece of work.
